# Orbital Speed of the ISS vs. Earth's Orbital Speed Around the Sun

## 1. Speed of the International Space Station (ISS)

The orbital speed of an object in a circular orbit is given by:

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}
$$

where:

- $G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{m}^3\text{kg}^{-1}\text{s}^{-2}$
- $M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\ \text{kg}$
- Earth's radius $R_E \approx 6371\ \text{km}$
- ISS altitude $h \approx 400\ \text{km}$

The orbital radius is:

$$
r = R_E + h
$$

$$
r = (6371 + 400)\,\text{km}
$$

$$
r = 6771\,\text{km}
= 6.771 \times 10^6\,\text{m}
$$

Substituting into the orbital speed formula:

$$
v_{\text{ISS}}
=
\sqrt{
\frac{(6.674\times10^{-11})(5.97\times10^{24})}
{6.771\times10^6}
}
$$

$$
v_{\text{ISS}}
=
\sqrt{5.885\times10^7}
$$

$$
v_{\text{ISS}}
\approx 7.67\times10^3\ \text{m/s}
$$

$$
\boxed{v_{\text{ISS}} \approx 7.67\ \text{km/s}}
$$

---

## 2. Earth's Orbital Speed Around the Sun

For a nearly circular orbit:

$$
v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}
$$

### Earth–Sun Distance

$$
r = 150\times10^6\ \text{km}
= 1.50\times10^{11}\ \text{m}
$$

### Earth's Orbital Period

$$
T = 365.25 \times 24 \times 3600
$$

$$
T = 3.156\times10^7\ \text{s}
$$

Substituting:

$$
v_E
=
\frac{2\pi(1.50\times10^{11})}
{3.156\times10^7}
$$

$$
v_E
\approx 2.99\times10^4\ \text{m/s}
$$

$$
\boxed{v_E \approx 29.9\ \text{km/s}}
$$

---

## 3. Comparison

| Object | Orbital Speed |
|----------|---------------|
| ISS around Earth | $7.67\ \text{km/s}$ |
| Earth around Sun | $29.9\ \text{km/s}$ |

The ratio of the speeds is:

$$
\frac{29.9}{7.67}
\approx 3.9
$$

This means Earth travels around the Sun about **3.9 times faster** than the ISS travels around Earth.

---

## Final Answer

- **ISS orbital speed:** **7.67 km/s**
- **Earth's orbital speed around the Sun:** **29.9 km/s**

**Conclusion:** Earth moves faster in its orbit around the Sun than the ISS moves around Earth. Earth's orbital speed is approximately **3.9 times greater** than the ISS's orbital speed.
