## 1. Speed of the International Space Station (ISS)

For a satellite in a circular orbit, the gravitational force provides the required centripetal force:

$$
F_g = F_c
$$

$$
\frac{GM_E m}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r}
$$

Cancelling $m$ from both sides and multiplying by $r$:

$$
\frac{GM_E}{r} = v^2
$$

Taking the square root:

$$
\boxed{v_{\text{ISS}} = \sqrt{\frac{GM_E}{r}}}
$$

where:

- $G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{m}^3\text{kg}^{-1}\text{s}^{-2}$
- $M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\ \text{kg}$
- $r = R_E + h$
- $R_E \approx 6371\ \text{km}$
- $h \approx 400\ \text{km}$

### Calculate the Orbital Radius

$$
r = R_E + h
$$

$$
r = 6371 + 400 = 6771\ \text{km}
$$

$$
r = 6.771 \times 10^6\ \text{m}
$$

### Calculate the Orbital Speed

$$
v_{\text{ISS}}
=
\sqrt{
\frac{(6.674\times10^{-11})(5.97\times10^{24})}
{6.771\times10^6}
}
$$

$$
v_{\text{ISS}}
=
\sqrt{5.885\times10^7}
$$

$$
v_{\text{ISS}}
\approx 7.67\times10^3\ \text{m/s}
$$

$$
\boxed{v_{\text{ISS}} \approx 7.67\ \text{km/s}}
$$
